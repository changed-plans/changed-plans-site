Refund policy

Short version: if a ticket sold here doesn't work, you get your money back — all of it. This page is commitments #6 and #7 of the Changed Plans Code, spelled out.

Last updated: November 3, 2026.

The pricing this policy sits on

Face value or less, never above. The only extra cost is a 14% buyer fee — and your first transaction is free.

1. If a ticket doesn't work — full refund (Code #6)

If you are turned away because a ticket sold on Changed Plans was invalid, you receive a full refund of everything you paid — the ticket price and the buyer fee. "Made whole" means whole. Open a dispute from your order page and tell us what happened at the door; a named human answers within two business days.

2. If the event is cancelled — at least face value back (Code #7)

If an event is cancelled and the organizer enables and authorizes refunds, you are refunded at least the face value of your ticket. Changed Plans' standard practice is to refund the full amount you paid, including the buyer fee, whenever the organizer's refund authorization lets the money flow back.

3. If the ticket never reaches you

Your payment is held by Changed Plans until the transfer to you is verified — it is never released to the seller before that (Code #5). If a verified transfer can't be completed, the order doesn't complete and your held payment is refunded in full.

4. Changed your mind?

All sales are final in the ordinary case — that's the nature of a fan-to-fan exchange, and it's why every listing shows its restrictions before you pay (Code #3). But plans change; that's why we exist. If your plans change after buying, you can list the ticket right back on Changed Plans at face value or less, held and verified the same way as any other listing.

5. How refunds happen

Refunds are issued to your original payment method through our payment processor, for the full refundable amount — we never keep the buyer fee on a refunded order. When a dispute leads to a refund, the decision and its reason are documented on the order (Code #8), and you'll see the order status move to refunded.

6. Sellers

If a sale is refunded because the ticket you provided was invalid, the payout for that order is voided (or, if already paid, must be returned) — sellers are paid for real tickets, not promises (Code #5). If an event is cancelled, neither side is out of pocket for a transaction that can't happen.
